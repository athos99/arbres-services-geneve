Safety first

What to do while we are on our way?

In the event of a broken tree or a threatening branch, the absolute priority is the safety of people. Here is what to do in a few simple steps.

1. Get people to safety

Evacuate occupants, children and animals, and stay away from the area where the tree could fall (at least 1.5 times its height).

2. Alert the emergency services

Public road, power cable or endangered building: call the police (117) or the fire brigade (118) while we are on our way.

3. Call us straight away

A split or uprooted tree can fall at any time. A short call is enough to mobilise our team and assess the priority.

Storms & severe weather

The emergency situations we handle

Wind, snow and ice, hail or internal rot in an unstable tree can turn a situation critical within hours. Here are the most common emergencies.

🌪️ Uprooted or torn-out tree

  • Lifted roots and destabilised ground
  • Hole to be backfilled and restored after the intervention
  • Removal of the roots and cutting of the stump if necessary

🌳 Split or hollow trunk

  • Longitudinal crack following an impact or internal rot
  • Risk of sudden failure high in the crown
  • Safety pruning, emergency cabling or felling with rigging

🪵 Hanging broken branch

  • Fractured main limb still attached to the crown
  • Risk of sudden fall even without wind
  • Rapid clearance by rope access or crane

Prevention is better than cure

Most emergencies can be anticipated. Regular maintenance, carried out at the right time, considerably reduces the risk of breakage during severe weather.

🌿

Sanitary pruning

Removing dead wood and dying branches before winter greatly reduces the risk of breakage during a storm.

⚓

Cabling

Dynamic securing of weakened limbs stabilises problem trees without condemning them.

🔍

Regular diagnosis

An annual visit identifies at-risk trees and allows action before the emergency: book a diagnosis visit.
