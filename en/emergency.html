Safety first

What to do while we are on our way?

In the event of a broken tree or a threatening branch, the absolute priority is the safety of people. Here is what to do in a few simple steps.

1. Get people to safety

Evacuate occupants, children and animals, and stay away from the area where the tree could fall (at least 1.5 times its height).

2. Alert the emergency services

Public road, power cable or endangered building: call the police (117) or the fire brigade (118) while we are on our way.

3. Cut the power if needed

If the tree touches an overhead line, switch off the power at the panel and do not touch anything. We will intervene in coordination with your electricity provider.

4. Call us straight away

A split or uprooted tree can fall at any time. A short call is enough to mobilise our team and assess the priority.

Storms & severe weather

The emergency situations we handle

Wind, snow and ice, hail or internal rot in an unstable tree can turn a situation critical within hours. Here are the most common emergencies.

🌪️ Uprooted or torn-out tree

  • Lifted roots and destabilised ground
  • Hole to be backfilled and restored after the intervention
  • Removal of the roots and cutting of the stump if necessary

🌳 Split or hollow trunk

  • Longitudinal crack following an impact or internal rot
  • Risk of sudden failure high in the crown
  • Safety pruning, emergency cabling or felling with rigging

🪵 Hanging broken branch

  • Fractured main limb still attached to the crown
  • Risk of sudden fall even without wind
  • Rapid clearance by rope access or crane

🏠 Tree on a building

  • Fall on a roof, a veranda or installations
  • Evacuation of occupants and protection of property
  • Felling with rigging in very restricted areas

🛣️ Tree blocking a road

  • Obstruction of a road, a driveway or a car park
  • Coordination with the police and local services
  • Rapid restoration of the public domain

⚡ Tree in contact with a cable

  • Collision with an overhead line or a telecommunication cable
  • Power cut-off and public safety guaranteed
  • Emergency pruning coordinated with the utility company

Our emergency services

An emergency is managed in two stages: secure the site immediately, then assess the long-term situation of the tree. Here is what we do in Geneva and the canton of Vaud.

Immediate securing of the site

Your safety comes first. We cordon off the area, keep passers-by away and neutralise immediate risks before any other operation.

  • Cordoning off the area: protection of people and property around the tree.
  • Clearing materials: securing branches that fell on a roof, a fence or a vehicle.
  • Photographic record: documentation of the damage for your insurance.

Emergency pruning by rope or crane

When complete removal is not necessary — a healthy tree that has lost main limbs — we carry out emergency pruning: removal of fractured wood, balancing of the silhouette and checking the balance of the crown.

  • Climbing arborist: work at height with no impact on the ground.
  • Crown balancing: rebalancing of the tree after the break.
  • Follow-up care: sanitary pruning and healing follow-up in the season.

Emergency felling with rigging

If the tree cannot be saved and endangers a place, we carry out high-precision felling: every branch and trunk section is lowered and guided to the ground with ropes, with no impact on neighbouring buildings.

  • Removal with rigging: no free fall onto the house or the road.
  • Difficult sites: in dense urban areas or on a property boundary.
  • Stump cutting: mechanical grinding of the stump if necessary.

Wood removal and site restoration

Once the danger is removed, we clear the site: branches are cut up, mulch or firewood is offered to you, and the ground is cleaned. The site is left tidy.

  • Transport up to 4 tonnes: removal of logs, branches and soil.
  • On-site chipping: production of mulch (BRF) for beds and vegetable gardens.
  • Local reuse: firewood and craft timber, according to your choice.

How does an intervention proceed?

A clear process, from the first call to the restoration of your property.

📞

1. Call and assessment

You describe the situation on the phone. We ask a few key questions (location, height, danger to people) to set the priority.

🚚

2. Travel to the site

We come on site with the appropriate equipment (crane, ropes, chainsaw) and assess the situation precisely.

🪢

3. Securing

Making the work area safe and immediately removing the risks (hanging branches, unstable trees).

🪚

4. Lasting solution

Emergency pruning, felling with rigging or keeping the tree: we propose the option that is safest and least demanding for the tree and for your property.

📄

5. Quote and follow-up

A clear quote prepared on site, with no surprises, and follow-up care for the tree in the following season.

🌿

6. Site restoration

Removal of the wood, cleaning of the site and, if you wish, planting a new replacement tree.

Prevention is better than cure

Most emergencies can be anticipated. Regular maintenance, carried out at the right time, considerably reduces the risk of breakage during severe weather.

🌿

Sanitary pruning

Removing dead wood and dying branches before winter greatly reduces the risk of breakage during a storm.

⚓

Cabling

Dynamic securing of weakened limbs stabilises problem trees without condemning them.

🔍

An annual visit identifies at-risk trees and allows action before the emergency: book a diagnosis visit.

Frequently asked questions about emergencies

How quickly do you intervene in an emergency?

We are available 7 days a week and intervene as a priority on the same day, depending on the weather and site access. A tree left standing is treated before conditions get worse.

Should the police or the authorities be informed before the intervention?

Yes. If the tree endangers a public road, a vehicle or a building, report the situation to the police (117) or the fire brigade (118) while we are on our way. No prior permit is required for work on private property.

What should I do while waiting for the tree expert?

Keep away from the area where the tree could fall, evacuate people and animals, cut the electricity in case of contact with a power line and never clear branches yourself: a split tree can topple without warning.

Is the emergency intervention billed separately?

The emergency quote is prepared on site after assessing the situation. The call-out is charged and the intervention remains clearly separated from planned maintenance work.

Do you also work at night and during holidays?

Yes, emergencies happen at night, at weekends and during holidays. Priority is given to situations that endanger people, buildings or traffic.

What happens to the branches and the felled wood?

The wood is pruned and cut on site or removed with our truck (up to 4 tonnes). It is turned into firewood, mulch (BRF) or timber, according to your choice.
